| 0:20 to 1:00 | Victor | Slide 2 | "On a Mars mission a message takes 4 to 24 minutes each way, so a question to the flight surgeon can take up to 48 minutes round trip. NASA's five spaceflight hazards often show up first as small shifts in everyday numbers: sleep, heart rate, reaction time. So the crew has to assess first and the ground confirms later. Space Vigil is that first look." |
| 1:00 to 1:40 | Adé | Live app, Today | "This is Elena, the commander, on mission day 30. One word, the reason, and one next step. Each tile is judged against her own 14-day pre-flight baseline, not the average: a resting heart rate of 95 is normal for most adults and wrong for someone whose baseline is 55." |
| 1:40 to 2:20 | Adé | Alerts, reaction-time alert | "Open an alert: it shows the rule that fired, the numbers, the published source and a checklist. At Mars it also says to act without waiting for a reply. And if a crew member rates themselves alert but the reaction test is slow, it says: trust the test, not the feeling." |
| 2:20 to 3:15 | Adé | Settings, solar particle event, day 25, then Today | "Now a solar storm. Today turns red for the whole crew. This replays real data: NASA's Curiosity rover measured this storm on 7 to 10 March 2012 on its way to Mars, read from the NASA Planetary Data System." |
